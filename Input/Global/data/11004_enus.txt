^000088The Truth Behind the Mercenary Uprising^000000

The Mercenary Uprising broke out in the year 781 and was suppressed the following year, in 782. On the surface, it has been described as an outburst of dissatisfaction caused by rising anxiety among mercenaries after the formation of the Guardian Corps. However, to this day, the exact motive, as well as the details of how the Republic Defense Force carried out the suppression, have never been properly clarified. Despite the Republic’s official statements, countless suspicions and speculations surrounding the uprising continue to swirl.
Accordingly, this paper will present a three-part series investigating the Mercenary Uprising.

The origins of the uprising trace back much further. The ancient scientific artifacts unearthed and successfully restored by the Great Sage Varmunt were mechanical creations capable of judgment and autonomous action. “Rekenber” devoted itself to producing mechanized soldiers using these relics, and before long, reached the stage of practical deployment.
However, Varmunt—unable to accept that the ancient relics he had restored and re-created were being used for the purpose of killing—immediately ended his cooperation with “Rekenber.” In the end, Rekenber’s First Development Division (hereafter referred to as “Rekenber”) had no choice but to proceed on its own. After years of repeated improvements, it finally produced the mechanized soldier known as the “Guardian.”

“Rekenber” sought to deploy the newly developed Guardians in real combat, and to that end, it attempted to lobby military figures, including the commander of the Republic Defense Force. Ultimately, a Guardian unit on the scale of two companies was newly formed within the mercenary corps attached to the Republic Defense Force, and the entire mercenary force began to stir.

In particular, fear spread through the ranks that their jobs might be taken by the Guardians, and open work slowdowns and refusals to follow orders began erupting across multiple units. Then an incident occurred: a Guardian on guard duty malfunctioned, resulting in the death of a platoon leader from the “Blade Fang Mercenary Corps.” Rekenber and the Defense Force tried to hush it up and cover it up, but the rumor spread through the entire mercenary corps in an instant, throwing the various mercenary groups into turmoil.

At last, the Blade Fang Mercenary Corps smashed Rekenber’s Guardians and broke away from the Defense Force. Mercenary corps across the duchy soon rose up and launched rebellions of their own, and the situation spiraled into a worst-case scenario. The insurgents destroyed Guardians with little resistance, looted several villages, and advanced toward Rekenber headquarters.

But the counterattack by Rekenber and the Republic Defense Force was swifter than expected. Rekenber committed a large-scale Guardian force, while the Republic Defense Force—small in number but elite—efficiently defeated the rebels by isolating and eliminating them in detail.

Driven onto the defensive, the rebels gathered their remaining forces and entrenched themselves at the mansion of the Great Sage “Varmunt.” Varmunt’s mansion and research facility were vast and sturdy like a fortress, filled with various mechanical devices designed to prevent outside intrusion, and also served as a place where restored ancient relics had been left stored—or abandoned.

The Guardian Corps and the Republic Defense Force immediately surrounded Varmunt’s mansion, and the rebels—offering little resistance—requested an armistice agreement with the Republic. The Republic Council, however, not only flatly refused negotiations, but also adopted a hardline stance that it would accept no surrender or offer of capitulation whatsoever.

The mercenary corps had once prided itself on absolute loyalty and obedience to superiors, yet after the collapse of negotiations, panic spread through the rebel ranks. Opinions split not only among the commanders but even among ordinary soldiers, and the siege took on an entirely new shape.

Many mercenaries began fleeing the mansion under cover of night, and most deserters were caught in the Republic Defense Force’s encirclement and met miserable ends. With their strength visibly diminished, the rebels attempted a final counterattack. Yet the Republic Defense Force, having obtained information in advance the night before, turned the tables with an ambush—and the mansion was opened by someone from within.

Once inside, the Defense Force and Guardian units carried out indiscriminate slaughter and destruction. Not only were the mercenaries who had plotted the uprising wiped out, but so too were the ancient relics Varmunt had spent his life striving to restore, along with his priceless works—reduced to ashes in fire and ruin.

With that brutal suppression, the Mercenary Uprising was brought to an end, but the backlash was far greater than expected. Public criticism mounted over the Republic Defense Force’s excessive use of force, and distrust toward Rekenber and the Council deepened. Soon, voices among the citizens demanding a full investigation into the truth behind the uprising grew louder.

In response, the Republic formed a Truth Investigation Committee and began an inquiry. However, the committee itself was composed largely of individuals affiliated with “Regenschirm,” along with members of the Republic Defense Force Inspectorate—making any genuine investigation or fact-finding difficult to expect.

Enraged, citizens demanded that the Consul hold a national referendum on the committee. With an overwhelming majority vote, the committee itself was reconstituted.

The newly formed committee uncovered that Rekenber had engaged in lobbying, and filed charges against several high-ranking Defense Force officials and multiple Council members involved. It further revealed that when the forces entered Varmunt’s mansion, a large quantity of ancient relics and Varmunt’s works were also leaked and taken out.

In the end, punishment and disqualification followed for those involved, and Prime Minister “Denier,” dealt a severe political blow, resigned in tandem with the Consuls with roughly ten months remaining in his term."
