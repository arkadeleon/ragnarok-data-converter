^778899Chapter 1: CURSE^000000

On the third day since he had sent Ahel away like that, Kshar stood before the Sphinx, one of the great ruins that occupies a large portion of Morroc. Sitting at the entrance of this colossal relic—its massive forepaws folded neatly before it like a cat loafing over freshly baked bread—Kshar paused to recall the events of the previous day.

He clearly remembered toasting drinks with Ahel. And then, he had endured two straight days that felt like hell. Among the personal rules Kshar strictly upheld were no smoking and no drinking. To catch even the faintest trace of poison, he kept strong scents and odors far from himself, and for similar reasons, he had long abstained from alcohol to maintain discipline. While he would occasionally allow himself a single light drink at formal gatherings or to suit the mood among comrades, why had he gone that far back then? Having rarely touched alcohol, he had only heard stories about the effects of heavy drinking—this was the first time he had ever experienced it firsthand.

“Why is the guild’s model straight-laced young man in this state? What’s with this smell of alcohol???”

A voice he heard amid the splitting headache and nausea. The calm, slightly high-pitched voice likely belonged to Ismil, who handled executive-level work. And then came another voice…

“He got beaten by booze. Not a bad sight, honestly. Pretty novel. Pathetic, too.”

Senior Zicrain. Kshar tried to lift his body to insist that he was fine, wriggling this way and that, but his body refused to obey him, heavy like waterlogged cotton. In the end, he simply gave up. He decided to lie there like a corpse until the dreadful hangover passed. Regretting his own foolishness for letting competitive pride drive him to keep pace with Ahel’s drinking, he instead focused bitterly on the voices around him.

“Didn’t this guy have a mission? It feels like it’s already been two days since he received it—can he really be lying around like this…?”
“Huh? A mission? What mission? Getting an assignment and then diving straight into a barrel of booze—he’s impressive in his own way.”
“This mission actually has something to do with you too, Ismil. You didn’t know?”

With an expression that clearly said This is news to me, Ismil shrugged toward Zicrain. Zicrain then began explaining the mission that the Guild Master had personally entrusted to Kshar. Of course, the two of them spoke freely, believing Kshar could not possibly hear them—but Kshar was grinding his teeth, carving every single word of their conversation into his mind.

“And the conclusion is…”

Kshar looked up at the sky. A faint sandy wind was blowing, yet the blue sky looked incredibly high and vividly clear. The hangover had eased somewhat, but a dull, throbbing headache still lingered.

“Uraaaaaaaah—!!!!”

Letting out a shout toward the sky—whether it was a battle cry or an outburst of anger was hard to say—Kshar leapt straight into the black maw of the Sphinx. The labyrinth within the Sphinx was so familiar to him that he could navigate it with his eyes closed. He pushed through the dungeon, striking down each Drainliar that flew at him and each restless servant of death that lunged at the living, delivering powerful, decisive blows one by one.

“What? Why would they make him do something so pointless? That’s just a test because they don’t want to teach skills for free. There’s no need to restock burial goods just for that.”

Twisting his body, Kshar slit the neck of a Requiem that charged from behind on his right with his Katar.

“Even if it were necessary, it might help newcomers just starting out. Still, the rank and the mission don’t match. That kind of job suits fresh recruits full of energy much better.”

Ismil’s voice echoed in his ears along with the headache. Kicking away the box Jerome was swinging created an opening. Kshar drove the blade of his Katar into the top of Jerome’s head, then immediately swung it toward a Drainliar flying in. Jerome, skewered through the crown of his skull and flung by Kshar’s strike, spewed black fluid as he flew back, crashing onto the stone floor together with the Drainliar.

“Why does the Master always pick on Kshar? He’s the elite among elites. Whether you like it or not, shouldn’t talent be used where it fits best? I just don’t understand the Master.”

“Ismil… that’s all part of the Master’s scheme. That resentment toward the Master is exactly why Kshar grew so strong in such a short time. He was desperate to prove himself, to show that he was strong enough to be acknowledged. The Master simply made very good use of that psychology. Anger is what turned the boy into an adult.”
