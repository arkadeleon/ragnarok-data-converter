^FA8072“Kras~ Kras~!”
Straining atop a tree as he struggled to set up a sunshade, Kras looked down at the voice calling him. There stood Senia, neatly dressed in a watery-blue one-piece, her dazzling silver hair scattering as it reflected the sunlight, waving up at him.

“Sen—! What do you think you’re doing?! If you’re exposed to direct sunlight, your skin will be ruined! Get inside, now!”
“Shut up, Kras! It’s my birthday today!”
“Yeah, Sen—I know. That’s why I’m putting up a sunshade so you can be outside. I mean… I like how you sparkle, but not right now. It’s too bright! It’s blinding!”
“Kras… do you not want to look at me? Is that it?”

Kras didn’t know what to do with Sen’s reaction. Ah, that little troublemaker. That stubborn brat. And yet, his beloved. Kras had always thought how wonderful it would be if Senia could, under the sun like other girls, pick flowers, dip her feet in a stream, even go on picnics.

But Senia was rare—born with hair, skin, everything, pure white. Her translucent skin would blush into a lovely pale pink when she grew angry or happy. It might look beautiful, but perhaps because of that skin, she was especially weak to sunlight. Ironically, her greatest enemy was the very sunlight that made her shine the most. If even a little sun touched her body, her snow-white skin would swell red at once. Outdoor play was something she couldn’t even imagine.

And so, just for her birthday, he’d climbed this tall tree and even set up a sunshade so she could spend time outside in warmth—yet Senia, knowing or not knowing, began hopping around under the tree, teasing him.

“Please~ Sen! Don’t shake the poles holding the sunshade~! Waaaah—no, no! Sen—! Hey! What if I get hurt?!”

Kras shouted himself hoarse, but Senia ignored it all with nothing more than a “hmph, hmph” and a toss of her head. Then she began hopping straight toward the center of the yard—where there was hardly any shade at all. Maybe Sen thought it was fun, but Kras’s insides burned. For her, even a brief exposure to sunlight could mean severe burns.

As Kras hurried down the tree, his eyes caught an even more unbelievable sight: Senia, unable to even keep her eyes open under the harsh sun, crumpling to the ground.

Kras all but slid down the tree, then jumped. He tore off his shirt and sprinted to Senia, who was sprawled in the yard, writhing. Wrapping her in his shirt, he rushed her into the house. Still hurting from the sudden glare, she squeezed her eyes shut and burrowed into his arms. As Kras soothed her, guilt rose in him. It was her birthday—he should have listened more. He even feared his own lack of consideration might ruin her day.

“Sen. Sen. It’s okay now. Open your eyes.”

^FA8072As Kras whispered softly by her ear, Sen cracked her eyes open and smiled faintly. How much must it have hurt? Her crimson eyes, still wet with tears, trembled as she gave him a tiny, smiling squint. Even if she tried to pretend she wasn’t in pain, Kras knew—those crimson eyes would see the world only dimly for a while. He kissed her forehead lightly, laid her on the bed, and held both her hands.
“Sen. Since I ruined your birthday… I’ll grant you one wish.”
“……”
“Why aren’t you saying anything?”
“……It’s not Kras’s fault, but…”
“But?”
“I’ll make a wish.”
That same bright, familiar voice. Kras felt grateful beyond words that the gloomy Sen, who had clung to him repeating “It’s my birthday…” had returned to herself. He eagerly hooked her pinky with his own.
“Go on, Sen. Make a wish. Look—everything’s ready. I’m fully prepared to swear I’ll grant it.”

Sen stared out the bright window for a long time, then slowly opened her mouth.

“Kras. I want a rose that opened its petals with the Goddess’s tears.”
“Okay. Got it. A rose that bloomed in the rain?”
Without thinking, Kras pressed his handprint and promised. But Senia, watching him smile, continued listing her conditions.

“The limit is three years, Kras. Within that time, you’ll give me a blood-red rose that bloomed on a rainy day. That’s my wish. Understood?”
“Yeah~ I just have to do it within three years, right? I’ll make it come true.”{/CL:25}^000000

And then, two years passed—and one day, Kras was being rejected by Senia for the fifth time.^000000

“Hahahahahaha!”
After hearing Kras’s story, Beren burst out laughing. He laughed for a long time, stopped to stare at Kras as if he couldn’t believe it, then started laughing all over again. Kras, embarrassed by Beren’s reaction, began laughing too. How long did they laugh? They laughed until they realized they were crouched in a pit, getting rained on, laughing—and then they laughed again.

“Aha… hahaha… So, Kras—before three years were up, did you… hahahaha… grant that wish? Hah… hahahaha…!”
Beren asked between gasps, and Kras smiled as if laughing—then suddenly turned serious.

“Right… This year makes three. And autumn’s almost over… What do I do…?”
“Yeah?”
“Hah—still, it’s lucky. At least I’m alive. Maybe I’ll run home like this and start growing roses.”

Kras smiled faintly at Beren.

“H… haha… That won’t happen. Unfortunately.”
“Be… Beren?”

Suddenly, a violent pain erupted in Kras’s abdomen. He stared at Beren, disbelief frozen on his face.

Why?
Why would Beren…?

Beren drove the blade he’d plunged into Kras’s belly even deeper and said:

“In a war fought in the name of the gods, the winner is the one still alive at the end.”

Kras gagged and vomited blood. Even as he retched, his eyes told Beren he still couldn’t believe it.

“B-but… that’s clearly Freyja’s symbol…”

At Kras’s words, Beren casually produced another relic he’d kept hidden. When he lifted a brilliant gold pendant, a small gasp—“Ah…”—escaped Kras.

Odin’s symbol.

Behind Kras’s stunned eyes, Beren took out the Freyja emblem at his waist, glanced at it as if it were nothing, then shoved it into a pouch at his belt. Inside the worn leather pouch were dozens of Freyja emblems, soaked in blood. Even the reddish-brown sash—what Kras had thought was an ally’s mark—when Beren unfolded it, the creased parts showed pale, whitish fabric beneath.

Stained with comrades’ blood…

“Wearing it like this, everyone relaxes. Heh.”

With a trembling right hand, Kras clutched the blade that pierced his abdomen. He reached his left hand toward Beren’s throat—but after losing so much blood, moving properly in the freezing rain was impossible. His body temperature began dropping rapidly, and the dagger aimed at Beren’s neck shook violently, having long since lost its target. Through his blurring vision, he saw Beren.

“You know… I’ve never praised the Goddess even once with my own mouth. Not back then, not now. Got it? Weakling. Falling for some girl like that.”

Beren’s venom didn’t reach Kras anymore. Kras only wanted to know why Beren had been his enemy. As if he understood that desire, Beren gave a short, bitter laugh and spoke.

“You’ll call it betrayal, won’t you? But I’ve never betrayed you—not even once. From the beginning, you and we were never meant to mix. Do you think you could understand? The hearts of those forced to live oppressed, giving ancestral land to foreigners? You—who force blind faith on others?! Friends? When was I ever your friend? You fools blinded by a vain Goddess!!!”

With that, Beren raised his sword and brought it down. In Kras’s eyes—collapsing without even the strength to resist—he saw Beren rummage through his clothes, pull out the Goddess’s emblem, pin it at his belt, and grin in satisfaction.

“This is all the will of the Chief God!!!!”

Beren’s mad voice rang in his ears—then was swallowed by the roaring rain.

The rain pouring from the sky drank Kras’s blood and began to spread swiftly. In his fading consciousness, Kras thought of Senia. The red droplets that had swallowed his blood looked like scattered petals. I have to keep… the promise… to Senia… I… have… to… Goddess… Freyja……

Senia thought the rain was unusually long this time. It wasn’t the usual passing shower. Leaning by the window, she stared outside. Everything was gray. The trees were darker, soaked by rain, and from the sky… red petals were falling.

Petals…?

Senia sprang to her feet.

Kras? Could it be Kras?

Before anyone could stop her, she threw open the door and bolted outside.

“Kras! Kras?! Are you back?”

Splashing through muddy water as she ran this way and that, she flinched at a red petal drifting down before her face and looked up.

Red petals.
Countless crimson rose petals swirled between the raindrops, drifting through the air.

“……………….”

^FA8072I came to deliver your love in your place.^000000

Senia reached her arms toward the sky. A petal fluttered down and settled on her fingertip. But at once, it drew a red line as it ran—down from her fingertip to her arm, from her arm to her chest, from her chest to her legs.

Another red streak traced down her cheek. It mixed with her tears and flowed downward.

The rose petals pouring from the sky spiraled toward her, gathering and settling all over her.

Staining the pure-white girl red—
like a single elegant rose blooming alone in the rain.
