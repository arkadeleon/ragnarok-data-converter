^778899Chapter 1: CURSE^000000

“…K-kill…… me…”

The hand gripping a longsword—its blade an unusually deep, pitch-black—trembled faintly. His armor, already more than half torn away, spoke for the ferocity of the battle he had endured. Blood seeped from his fingertips; his strength should have given out, his weapon should have slipped from his grasp—yet his hand would not let go of the sword.

His voice, thick with tears, came again.

“…Please… just stop now… kill me.”

One of his knees buckled, and he sank onto the icy floor. Using the sword as a support, he struggled to raise himself again—his face plainly showing how overwhelming the pain was as it swallowed his body. The one who had always shown me what “light” was with a bright smile. The proud figure who had been acknowledged by the Valkyrie of Battle and became a Lord Knight… none of that dignity remained in him now.

I pointed at him with one of my Katar’s blades.

“…Haa… With your own hands… kill… me…”
“Do you truly want to die?”

At my flat, emotionless question, he forced a smile—his face smeared with tears and blood.

“Haa… haa… yeah…”

And yet, against those words, his blade drove sharply into my chest. I knocked the sword aside with both Katar blades and slipped backward. With eyes brimming with tears, that bastard—Ahel—stared at his own sword.

This can’t be.
You don’t mean it.

His expression toward me said exactly that.

“I’m sorry… I’m sorry…”

A wind mixed with tears blew through. I never knew Ahel was the type to cry so much. Nor had I ever seen him desire something so desperately. I had always stood at the boundary between life and death. I was the one who had mercilessly delivered death even to those who begged to be spared.

But this time was different.

I couldn’t grant Ahel’s wish as he begged to be killed.

“No!!!”

With Ahel’s anguished shout, his sword flew in, tracing a black arc. He should have collapsed from exhaustion by now, but his body—having lost its will—seemed to be dragged along by the sword itself, burning up whatever life he had left.

The longer this standoff lasted, the more dangerous it became for Ahel.
There was no time.

If only I could separate that sword from him…!

A sword…?
Right. There was a way.

The cursed black blade came again, thrusting sharply toward my heart.

KRAK! KRRRCH!!

“Khk!”
“Kshar—!!”

Ahel’s shocked face entered my vision in slow motion. His hand still hadn’t released the sword. I pulled the blade lodged in my chest even deeper in.

A tearing pain—my heart convulsing violently—writhing.
I reached out toward Ahel, now closer than ever.

Ahel’s trembling hand…
The sword finally dropped from him.

I saw him calling my name in a shaking voice, but soon my vision dimmed into darkness, and Ahel’s voice drifted farther away inside my head.

That was it.

Even this damn sword couldn’t keep playing with a corpse.

“Kshar.”
“Did you summon me, Master?”

A dark room—one no one dared enter unless chosen. A place said to conceal half of all the continent’s secrets. Deep underground, in the hidden chamber of the Assassin Guild that stood solemnly amid a raging sandstorm, Kshar offered formal respect to the one who had called for him after so long.

“I heard you met the Valkyrie of Battle not long ago.”
“…That’s not exactly rare, is it?”
“Not so common that it’s like a stone rolling around on the road, either.”
“…Are you calling that a joke…”
“It’s not a joke. I’m proud of you, that’s all. You’re modest for no reason. Sometimes, let people who praise you feel rewarded, too.”
“So what is it this time? You’re not going to bring up Valhalla again, are you?”
“No, of course not. But I heard you haven’t received a single mission since becoming an Assassin Cross, so I thought I’d personally give you one.”

Kshar eyed the Assassin Guild’s leader—who continued speaking with an easy grin—with suspicion.

“…What kind of busywork are you trying to dump on me now…”
“Hahahaha! Busywork, you say. My reputation must be in the gutter. Do I look like the kind of man who’d give you ‘busywork’? You’re the one joking.”
“Then what is it?”
“Tsk, tsk. You never fix that attitude. You should say, ‘Give your command.’ Kshar… Just because you became an Assassin Cross doesn’t mean you can get cheeky with me.”

Kshar lowered his gaze to the floor, vowing that if he ever got the chance, he’d cut off the hair this man always took pride in—neatly combed and flowing down his back.

“Give your command.”
“That’s more like it. A mission. I’ll give you a few items—go hide them somewhere among the relics in the Pyramid. Anyplace. Done!”
“…”
“Hoo, isn’t it a magnificent mission? It’d be hard for anyone but you. This is for the future of our Assassin Guild.”
“…………”

“Kshar.”

Kshar, too dumbfounded to speak, merely bowed and turned to leave—only for the Guild Master to stop him.

“You should say, ‘Yes, Master!’”
“……Y-yes… Master.”

Bang.

“Such a shy brat…”
“Will it be all right?”

At some point—without anyone noticing—Huy was standing beside the Guild Master.

“Hm? What will?”
“You always seem to bully Kshar. I imagine his dissatisfaction must be piling up.”
Wearing a quiet, worried tone, Huy spoke—only to find he could do nothing but pity Kshar at the Guild Master’s next words.

“Because it’s fun.”

BAM!

“Damn it!”

CRASH—BANG!

“Damn it! Damn you to hell!!!”

SMACK!

“Which bastard was that?!”

Kshar—kicking a chair, stomping, throwing a tantrum—whirled around, killing intent flaring, after someone struck the back of his head.

And froze.

“Z-Zieg… senior…!”
“What are you doing?”
“…I’m sorry.”
“The bar master says you’re being so loud he can’t even taste his drink—and he’s asking if he’s supposed to run a business scared out of his mind.”
“I’m sorry. I didn’t think the sound would carry.”

Zicklein patted Kshar’s shoulder as the latter stood pale and stiff, then continued.

“I know the Guild Master has a special fondness for you…”
“……”
“But that doesn’t mean you can destroy the Guild’s property whenever you like.”

Perhaps thanks to Zicklein’s calming, Kshar steadied himself and slowly looked over what he’d done. A wooden chair lay in the corner with one leg half snapped off, and a table had been overturned.

“I trust you’ll clean up the chair on the floor and the overturned table yourself, but…”

Zicklein paused, then slowly raised one foot.

“And about you calling me a ‘bastard’—”
“Kshar! You in here? Huh—?”

A savior!

Kshar screamed that inwardly as a man opened the half-ajar door and stepped in. Zicklein lowered the leg he’d raised and straightened up. As Ahel entered, looking around in confusion, Zicklein gave Ahel’s head a couple of light pats, then left the room without a word—though he didn’t forget to send Kshar a silent warning with his eyes on the way out.

A basement tavern in the desert city of Morroc—known only to those “in the know.”
In one corner, Ahel sat with a smile so bright it was almost burdensome, aimed straight at Kshar across from him. With pale platinum hair and white skin, Ahel’s wide grin doubled the weight of that “brightness” on Kshar.

“Why did Zieg hyung lift one leg up and down earlier?”
“You don’t need to know. Anyway—why are you here, all the way out here?”
“Can’t I come see you? And Kshar—when your friend’s talking, could you at least look at the person speaking? What is this—conversation? It’s just me doing a monologue.”
“I’m listening properly and answering properly, so it’s not a monologue. Why are you really here?”

No matter how close Ahel was to him, he was still a Knight. For a Knight to come and go freely through a hidden underground hideout of Assassins wasn’t exactly easy, given their temperament.
Ah—of course, there were exceptions.

As that thought clicked into place, Kshar lifted his head and told Ahel with his eyes to spit it out.

“Now you’re talking. It’s nothing big—I just need to go somewhere.”
“A trip? Do you need a companion?”
“You’ll come with me?”

At Ahel’s hopeful gaze, Kshar nearly answered at once—“Alright. I’ll go.”—but then remembered the mission he’d received moments earlier.

“…I have a mission.”

Ahel showed a brief look of disappointment, but quickly recovered his usual smile and continued.

“Then that tantrum earlier—don’t tell me it was because of the mission? You hated it that much?”
“Yeah. I did. It’s not the first time! That bastard!”

Kshar downed what remained in his cup in one gulp and slammed it onto the table.

“Figures… Still, thanks to that, you and I got to meet.”
“……Hoo…”
“An Assassin plucking Picky feathers… That really shocked me back then.”
“…Haa… stop it.”
“And come to think of it—an Assassin hauling cut bamboo around isn’t common either, right?”
“…Hoo… I said stop…”
“Oh, right. And why did you steal Sohee’s clothes? Don’t tell me that was a mission too?”
“Didn’t you hear me say stop, you bastard?! Fine! Yeah—me! I was the one crouched down plucking Picky feathers, and your foot kicked me and sent me tumbling! Me! I cut bamboo, dried it in the shade, and split it along the grain! Me! I stole scraps of clothing off monsters, ironed them stiff, hung them up to dry, and made them look like new clothes before offering them up! Why!! Did you want to tell everyone in the world so badly!!! Hh… hh…”

Maybe he’d gotten too worked up. After blurting out all that in one breath, Kshar was left panting—while Ahel only let out his usual little “heh” laugh.

“Yeah. Don’t bottle it up. Holding it in just makes you sick. Doesn’t it feel better to say it out loud?”

As Ahel smiled innocently and lightly tapped the table, inviting him to sit, Kshar thought:
Right. Ahel always soothed me. Just like now…
Just like now?

Like now—how, exactly?

Kshar glanced around. He felt the stares, and only then realized it.

Ahel had baited him into loudly confessing every shameful thing from his past—things he absolutely should not have broadcast to everyone.
And he’d almost mistaken the one responsible for it as someone he should be grateful to.

Oh, Odin—!

The moment Kshar’s thoughts reached that point and he began to growl low to snap at Ahel—

Ahel’s hand patted Kshar’s head.

“Do your mission well. And by the time you get back, I’ll be a Lord Knight. What a shame you won’t be there with me at that glorious moment.”
“What?”

Kshar, about to grab Ahel’s wrist to stop him from patting his head like a child, froze at the unexpected words.
So that’s why he said he had somewhere to go…

“If I’m going to hang out with an Assassin Cross, shouldn’t I at least be a Lord Knight? I worked really hard, you know. Now—tell me to have a safe trip, won’t you?”
“Y-yeah. H-have a safe trip…”
“Alright—got my friend’s heartfelt send-off… So, should we drink today? Kshar. Don’t think you’re sleeping easy tonight, got it? Master! Fill it up with strong ale!!”

Ignoring Kshar, still dazed, Ahel’s boisterous order kicked off a drinking session that—just as he promised—soaked Kshar in alcohol until dawn.
