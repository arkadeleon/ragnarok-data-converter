^000088The Tyrant Schmitz^000000

In A.W. 511, Walter von Schmitz ascended the throne of the Rune-Midgard Kingdom as Grand King at the age of thirty-two.
His father was Walter von Braham, and his grandfather was Walter von Eik.

Eik, Schmitz’s grandfather and a prince of the realm, harbored deep resentment over the line of succession and took part in the so-called “Prince’s Rebellion” of A.W. 440, leading his private troops into war. He was defeated by Prince Rakan and slain while attempting to flee.
At the time, Eik had two sons: Braham, Schmitz’s father, and his elder brother, Ogart. After the failure of Eik’s rebellion, the Walter family’s standing within the kingdom diminished drastically, and they came to be regarded as a second-rate royal house, pushed aside in the struggle for royal power. Ogart, Braham’s elder brother, died young at the age of eight due to illness, leaving Braham as the sole heir of the Walter bloodline.

From childhood, Braham was sharp-witted and perceptive, quickly grasping his precarious position. More than anyone, he burned with ambition to restore his family.
It did not take long for him to transform from the “son of a traitor” into a “capable young prince.”
Braham grew into an outstanding figure even among princes of his generation, and despite lingering concerns about his lineage, he was ultimately recognized as a legitimate claimant to the throne.

Even so, the crown remained far from Braham’s reach. His support base was weak, and his position in the line of succession was distant. Yet the ambitious young Braham never despaired. Through two political marriages, he gradually built a foundation of support within the royal court. Though he had long been unlucky in matters of offspring, at the age of thirty-eight Braham finally fathered a son with his second wife—Walter von Schmitz.

Unable to relinquish his desire for royal power, Braham sent his son Schmitz to be adopted into the first royal house, the Geoborg family, when the boy was just two years old. Raised as a son of the Geoborg family, Schmitz was recognized as a legitimate heir to the throne at the age of sixteen. Thereafter, Prince Schmitz accumulated numerous achievements and steadily rose in the line of succession. However, persistent suspicions remain that unseen machinations between the Geoborg and Walter families lay behind his rapid ascent.

In A.W. 509, following the death of King Ozel, the royal court was thrown into turmoil. The first in line to the throne was Prince Gunther of the Löwenburg family, while the second was Prince Max of the Richard family, the late king’s younger brother. Schmitz stood third in succession; however, he was a prince of the foremost royal house, Geoborg. Moreover, Gunther was too young, and Max lacked a solid base of support.

For more than a year, court ministers and the various royal houses argued indecisively over the matter of succession. At last, in A.W. 511, they elevated Schmitz as Grand King.

At first, King Schmitz appeared to govern smoothly, but little by little his true nature began to emerge. He launched numerous overseas ventures, driving many court officials out of the palace and filling his inner circle with flatterers and loyal retainers. Near the capital, he constructed a massive arena, where adventurers slaughtered demons for public spectacle, winning the favor of the populace.

Schmitz’s popularity rose by the day, and soon he wielded absolute royal authority. He ordered the palace rebuilt and began spending tax revenue as though it were water. Endless banquets and festivities eventually brought the royal treasury to ruin. Around that time, Braham von Walter—Schmitz’s biological father—passed away. Rumors circulated openly within the court about both his death and the long-kept secret that he had been the king’s true father, and these whispers eventually reached Schmitz himself.

Upon learning the truth of his birth and his father’s fate, Schmitz came to harbor both hatred toward the Geoborg family and a twisted mix of love and resentment toward the Walter family. From that moment on, he embarked on a cruel, blood-soaked path of revenge. His first target was the Löwenburg royal house—revenge against Grand King Rakan, who had slain his grandfather Walter von Eik.

The illustrious Löwenburg family, which had produced many kings over the generations, was devastated by Schmitz’s unilateral purge. Its members were either executed or forced to flee the country, and those already abroad were formally exiled.

Next came the Walter family. In the name of restoring their honor and reviving the house, Schmitz carried out blatant appointments—and went further still, altering the very foundation of the state by reforming the system of royal succession. Previously, kings had been chosen based on rankings determined by a council of royal house heads and high-ranking nobles. Schmitz abolished this council and replaced it with a designation system, allowing the reigning monarch to name their successor during their lifetime.

Yet despite his boundless favoritism toward the Walter family, Schmitz showed cold indifference toward the Geoborg family. He came to favor nobles over royal kin, appointing them to high offices—most of whom were selected not for ability, but for loyalty and skill in flattery.

Still unsatisfied with the existing royal palace, Schmitz ordered the construction of a colossal fortress beneath the slopes of Mount Myornir, in the Glast Heim region. Enormous national funds were poured into building the new Holy Castle of Glast Heim—three times the size of the Prontera Palace—and to cover the costs, the court began squeezing the populace mercilessly.

After a massive construction effort spanning more than five years, the new castle was completed. Upon touring it, Schmitz impulsively ordered the royal court relocated to Glast Heim. In a single day, the status of Prontera Palace—symbol of royal authority and capital of the Rune-Midgard Kingdom for over five centuries—collapsed.

From the new castle, Schmitz severed nearly all communication with the outside world, indulging solely in decadence and pleasure while extracting crushing taxes from citizens and adventurers alike. In his later years, it is said he sought eternal life through the power of demons, abducting people and offering them as living sacrifices.

In A.W. 529, Grenholm von Geoborg—Schmitz’s cousin—launched a coup d’état. Armed rebels, rallying under the banner of overthrowing a tyrant, swept through the provinces, defeating royal forces with unstoppable momentum and eventually surrounding Glast Heim Castle.

After several weeks of siege, the rebels breached the castle—only to be met by demon legions summoned by Schmitz himself. Despite staggering losses, the rebel army under Grenholm’s command ultimately captured Glast Heim. Schmitz was taken prisoner, but by then he was no longer sane, his mind broken by years of demonic rituals and self-inflicted experiments in pursuit of immortality.

He was confined within a tower of Glast Heim, where his imprisonment began. Several months later, when the captain of the guard entered his chamber, Schmitz had vanished without a trace. After an extensive search and investigation, it was concluded that he had used an inexplicable demonic power to hide himself within the mirror in his room.

Grenholm, crowned Grand King, left Glast Heim Castle abandoned and ordered the royal court’s return to Prontera, reinstating it as the capital of the Rune-Midgard Kingdom."
